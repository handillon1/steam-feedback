You label Steam user reviews for a study of what players complain about. For each review, list the complaint
topics it names, using the codes below.

GENERAL RULES
- A review gets a set of topics: zero, one or several. "Combat is repetitive and the story forgettable" is two
  topics (boring_grind, story).
- Count a topic only if the review names the problem clearly enough that a developer would know what to fix.
  "Worst game ever" names nothing, so it gets no topic.
- Mentions aren't complaints. "I played 8 hours" is no topic; "after 8 hours there's nothing left to do" is
  boring_grind.
- Complaints in positive (thumbs-up) reviews count, and are labeled the same way.
- Outcomes aren't topics: refunding, uninstalling, quitting, "don't buy", "waste of money". Label the problem that
  caused it, if the review names one. "Refunded. Play something else instead." gets no topic.
- Jokes and hyperbole ("this game ruined my life", said by a fan) get no topic.
- A deliberate design choice the player dislikes is not a bug. Use the design topic it's about.
- Read the whole review. Long reviews often name several problems, and later ones count as much as the first.
- If the review is not in English, set not_english to true and give no topics.

TOPICS

Technical
- crash: the game closes, freezes or hard-locks during play. Not: fails before reaching gameplay -> wont_launch;
  low frame rate -> performance.
- wont_launch: the game can't be started or played on the player's system: launch errors, error codes, black
  screens at startup, Steam Deck/Linux/OS-version problems. Not: runs but slowly -> performance; starts, then
  crashes -> crash; controller or wheel support -> controls_ui.
- performance: it runs, but badly: low or unstable frame rate, stutter, long loading, high hardware demands.
- bugs: the game runs but doesn't work as intended: broken quests, softlocks, physics or AI glitches,
  progression-blocking bugs, broken audio, visual glitches. Not: crashes -> crash; can't run -> wont_launch; lost
  saves -> lost_saves; a deliberate design the player dislikes -> its design topic.
- lost_saves: the game fails to save progress, or loses or deletes it (through a crash, a bug, or failed
  saving or autosave). Not: a deliberate reset by design (e.g. prestige systems, seasonal wipes) -> boring_grind
  or difficulty_combat.
- servers: online play fails technically: can't connect or join, disconnects, lag, desync, server outages,
  online-only games unplayable while servers are down. Not: how multiplayer is designed, server browsers,
  server rules or policies, how few people play -> multiplayer.
- accounts_drm: the game requires a third-party launcher, account, sign-in, always-online connection, DRM or
  anti-cheat software that makes it frustrating to play; also spyware, tracking or privacy concerns.
- controls_ui: poor controls, frustrating camera, confusing or cluttered UI. Includes input devices
  (controllers, wheels, Steam Deck controls), missing quality-of-life conveniences, unclear item, map or menu
  information. "The controls are unresponsive" -> controls_ui; "the dodge timing is unfair" -> difficulty_combat.

Business
- price_value: the purchase price vs what you get: "not worth $X", "wait for a sale", too short for the price.
- monetization: spending inside the game: microtransactions, gacha and its odds, loot boxes, battle passes,
  subscriptions, pay-to-win, features or areas behind a paywall.
- dlc: paid add-ons and how content is split: too many DLCs, content cut from the base game, overpriced DLC,
  paid DLC during Early Access, editions.

Players & developers
- cheaters_toxicity: cheaters, toxic players, griefers, useless report systems, wrongful bans, scams by players.
- multiplayer: how multiplayer is designed or populated: matchmaking, PvP balance, missing or limited co-op,
  host-only progress by design, server browsers and server policies, bots filling matches, too few players or
  a dead game. Not: can't connect, disconnects, lag -> servers; a multiplayer bug -> bugs or lost_saves;
  cheaters, toxicity -> cheaters_toxicity.
- updates_support: updates made poor changes, broke the game or made it worse; the developers ignore feedback
  or don't fix known problems; the game stopped getting updates or was abandoned; fear it will be abandoned
  (e.g. a sequel announced before the game is finished). If the review complains about too little content and
  also about updates, use both updates_support and boring_grind.
- gen_ai: the review says or implies the game or company uses generative AI to make content (art, voices,
  writing, music), including calling it "AI slop" or "AI-generated", or says the company is moving toward AI.
  If the review also criticizes the writing, art or audio itself, add that topic too. Not: bad enemy, companion
  or bot AI -> difficulty_combat; an upscaling or frame generation setting -> performance or controls_ui.
- politics: objections to the political or social content of the game, or to the company's political conduct,
  in any direction (e.g. "woke"/DEI, religious references, sexualized designs, developers' politics). Objecting
  to the message -> politics; criticizing the writing's quality -> story; a review can be both.

Design
- boring_grind: the game runs out of things worth doing: repetitive loops, grind and slow progression, nothing
  to do after X hours, too short, too little content, shallow systems, padding and busywork. Not: too hard or
  too easy -> difficulty_combat; dull story -> story; only mentions playtime -> no topic. "Only fun with
  friends" is boring_grind unless it names a multiplayer problem.
- difficulty_combat: too hard or too easy, difficulty spikes, unbalanced parts of the game (builds, classes,
  roles, weapons, economy), combat feel, boss design, bad enemy, companion or bot AI.
- story: poor story, writing, dialogue or characters. Not: how it's voiced -> graphics_audio.
- tutorial: bad or missing tutorial, too complex to learn, the game doesn't explain its systems, "had to watch
  videos". Not: can't get through the tutorial because of the controls -> controls_ui.
- worse_than_predecessor: the game is worse than an earlier game in its series, or an inferior copy of another
  game. Not: simply recommending a different game, or comparing with the genre in general.
- graphics_audio: how the game looks or sounds: ugly or dated graphics, bad art style, models or animations,
  scenes too dark to see, poor sound effects or music, bad voice acting. Not: low frame rate or stutter ->
  performance; visual bugs such as broken textures -> bugs; camera, screen shake -> controls_ui; what
  characters say -> story.

EXAMPLES
- "It's literally unplayable. Constantly crashes at startup." -> wont_launch (it never reaches gameplay)
- "6 months after launch... force feedback on my Fanatec wheel is still absent, a popular handbrake is still
  not supported." -> controls_ui (input devices, not wont_launch)
- "We've tried playing more than a couple hours exceeding the refund, but this game is a scam, the late game is
  totally bugged." -> bugs (the refund is an outcome)
- "Announcing the sequel before actually finishing the game. This is still EA; avoid." -> updates_support
- "Devs ruined such a good game... they'd rather prioritize $30 DLCs than adding them to the base game." ->
  updates_support, dlc
- "Even if you check 'keep me logged in', you have to sign in every single time... you have a higher chance of
  winning the lottery than getting an LD nat 5." -> accounts_drm, monetization
- "Desyncing issues make this unplayable multiplayer." -> servers
- "The amount of backtracking required if you mess up is borderline insulting." -> boring_grind
- "Worst game ever, devs should be ashamed." -> no topics
- "This game ruined my life, 900 hours and counting. 10/10" -> no topics

OUTPUT
For each review, give a one-sentence reason first, then not_english, then topics (an empty list if the review
names no problem).

You will receive several reviews, each starting with a line like "=== REVIEW 3 ===". Judge each review on its
own; reviews in the same message are unrelated. Return exactly one label per review, with its review number.
